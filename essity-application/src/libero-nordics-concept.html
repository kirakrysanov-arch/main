03 · First 90 days
First 90 days

Listen first. Then plan together. Then deliver.

A brand manager who arrives with all the answers usually has the wrong ones. My plan is to earn trust in the four markets quickly, build one fact base, and show a first result within the quarter.

Days 1–30

Listen

  • Meet sales, customer marketing and the brand team in all four markets
  • Store and e-commerce visits in each country, with sales colleagues
  • Learn the P&L, price ladder and SKU contribution per market
  • Get hands-on with Nielsen and our internal reporting
Output: a one-page view per market of what is working, what is not and what the teams need from brand.
Days 31–60

Learn

  • Review the last 12 months of activations: what moved share and what didn't
  • Map the innovation pipeline against each market's customer calendar
  • Find assortment quick wins: tail SKUs, gaps, pack-price architecture
  • Agree shared KPIs with Sales and Customer Marketing
Output: a Nordic scorecard and a shortlist of three priorities everyone has signed up to.
Days 61–90

Lead

  • Deliver one quick-win activation together with one market
  • Draft the annual activation and business plan with local teams
  • Prepare the trade story for the next launch
  • Set the monthly performance rhythm and corrective-action routine
Output: a first measurable result, and next year's plan built bottom-up with the markets.
What I'll ask forAccess to Nielsen and sales data from day one, a store-visit buddy in each market, and 30 minutes a month with each country lead to review performance together.

05 · Concept sketch
Concept sketch · August–September

Ready for
daycare.
Ready to explore.

Across the Nordics, many little ones start daycare in August. It is a big step for the child and an emotional one for parents. Libero can be the brand that makes the first weeks feel easier, with practical help and a smile.

Insight to validate"I'm not worried about the nappy. I'm worried about whether my child is comfortable and happy when I'm not there."

1In store

A "daycare start" shelf moment with key customers: pants for active toddlers, multipacks and wipes together, with a simple checklist on pack or shelf.

2Daycare kit

A printable and digital "first week" checklist and name labels, co-created with Nordic daycare staff for credibility.

3Social & content

Short, honest films of real first days in all four countries. Local voices, one shared look and feel.

4Retail media & CRM

Target parents of children around one year old through retailer loyalty data and Libero's own channels.

Business goalGrow share in the pants segment during the transition to daycare, when routines and brands are re-chosen.
How we measureValue share in pants, sell-out uplift vs. baseline at participating customers, reach and engagement.
Pilot firstTest with one customer in one market, read results, then scale to the Nordics the next year.

06 · Launch & assortment
Launch & assortment playbook

Launches that land, and a range that earns its space.

−9 to −6 months
Prepare

Local business case, price point and listing ambition per market

−6 to −3 months
Sell in

Trade presentation with a clear consumer story and shelf proposal

−3 to 0 months
Get ready

Phase-out plan for old SKUs, shopper materials, content toolbox

Launch
Activate

Joint activation with key customers, retail media and PR

+1 to +6 months
Track & fix

Weekly early signals, monthly review and agreed corrective actions

Launch KPIWhy it matters
Weighted distributionAre we where shoppers buy? The first thing to fix if it is low.
Rate of saleDo shoppers pick it when it is there? Tells us if the proposition works.
Repeat & incrementalityAre we growing the brand, or just moving sales between our own SKUs?
Value share & price indexAre we holding our price position vs. competitors and private label?

Assortment rules of thumb

  • Review the tail every year: SKUs that bring little profit and no strategic role make room for new ones
  • Plan phase-in and phase-out together, so we never leave a gap on shelf or a stock write-off
  • Keep a clear pack-price ladder per market, from trial packs to value packs
  • Use customer-specific ranges where they add sales, not complexity
  • Bring local insight back to the brand team for the next innovation and business case
