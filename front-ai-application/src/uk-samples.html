Account brief · 1 / 2

DC Thomson

A 120+ year-old Dundee publisher putting money into marketing, technology, data and AI to grow digital subscriptions. Its subscribers currently have to work out which of several phone lines to call.

£41 Msubscription revenue (FY to Mar 2025, +1%)
50,000+paying subscribers to its regional news titles
4+separate phone lines for subscribers, by title and topic
9–5shop contact centre hours, Mon–Fri

What we noticed

  • Many front doors. The Courier digital (0800 029 4956), Press and Journal digital (0800 029 4955), home delivery (01382 575427) and DC Thomson Shop magazines (0800 904 7260) each have their own number. The customer has to choose the right one.
  • Contact reasons that repeat. Even with a 4.8/5 Trustpilot score, the complaints are the same few topics: missing or late issues, billing frequency, refunds and early renewal reminders. These are ideal for self-service.
  • Evenings and weekends. Shop subscribers are served 9–5 on weekdays. Readers who notice a missing Saturday paper have nowhere to go until Monday.
  • Self-service exists already. Subscription management runs through Air Business (renewed on a 3-year contract) and DC Thomson's own help section. The tools are there, but customers aren't steered towards them.

The opportunity

One “How can we help?” front door for every DC Thomson title. It steers missing issues, billing and delivery questions to self-service around the clock, and saves agents' time for cancellations and win-back calls, where a human conversation protects revenue.

Why it fits their strategy

  • Supports the stated investment in technology, data and AI with an affordable layer, not a replatforming project
  • Works with Air Business and the existing help pages; no rip-and-replace
  • Protects the £41 M subscription line: fewer frustrated calls about missing issues means less churn
  • One set of channel rules can be reused across news, magazines and Beano

Sources: Press Gazette & Scottish Financial News (FY to 31 Mar 2025 results), InPublishing / Air Business (subscriptions contract), DC Thomson and Press and Journal contact pages, Trustpilot (DC Thomson Shop). Facts to be re-checked before sending.

Account brief · 1 / 2

Iliffe Media Group

A family-owned regional publisher in the middle of a big integration. In 2026 it took on 30 Tindle titles through a joint venture with the Fowler family, and plans to make building digital subscriptions for those titles a priority.

30former Tindle titles added in 2026 (Wales, West Country, Surrey, Hampshire, Isle of Man)
30,000+digital subscribers before the Tindle deal
11counties served across England, plus the new regions
3+support paths: subscriptions site, title pages, PageSuite for e-editions

What we noticed

  • Integration moment. Readers of 30 titles are about to get new subscription offers, a new app and new support routes. Habits form in the first weeks, so this is the time to set them.
  • Many products, many questions. Digital, app, print vouchers, e-mail editions and three group packages (Friends & Family, Business Standard, Business Premium) all generate different questions.
  • Support is split. Digital-edition problems go to PageSuite, subscriptions to Iliffe Media Subs, and print delivery to individual titles. Readers have to know which is which.
  • A digital-first push. Growing digital subscriptions is the stated priority, and each subscriber who can't get help quickly is a churn risk.

The opportunity

Give all 30 new titles one front door for reader help from day one, set up per title from a single console. Readers get the fastest route to app log-in, vouchers or delivery help, and the subscriptions team can absorb 30 titles without growing at the same rate.

Why it fits their strategy

  • Scales the integration: one set of rules, reused across 30+ local brands
  • Supports the digital-subscription push by steering readers to the app and self-service
  • Vendor-neutral: works next to PageSuite and the existing subscriptions platform
  • Live analytics show leadership how each title's readers behave from the first week

Sources: Press Gazette, Cambrian News, RadioToday (Tindle sale, Mar 2026), KentOnline, IPSO annual statement, Iliffe Media and Iliffe Media Subs pages. Facts to be re-checked before sending.
