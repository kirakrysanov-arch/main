DC Thomson: one front door for every subscriber

A Dundee publisher with more than a century of history and a transformation agenda: more digital subscriptions, and investment in technology, data and AI. Its subscribers still have to pick between separate phone lines depending on which title they read and what went wrong.

£41 Msubscription revenue, FY to Mar 2025 (+1%)
50,000+paying subscribers to regional news titles
4separate subscriber phone lines, by title and topic
£31 Mpre-tax profit; still investing in tech, data and AI

Why now

  • Transformation has an owner. Ella Dolphin became Deputy CEO in May 2025 with a remit for M&A and transformation across the portfolio.
  • Subscriptions are the growth engine, and behind plan. Regional titles passed 50,000 subscribers, short of the 75,000-by-2025 target. Retention matters as much as acquisition.
  • Open to partner tech. In May 2026 they added AI narration (BeyondWords × Pugpig) to the Stylist and Press and Journal apps. They buy focused tools; they don't build everything themselves.
  • Service is a team they're investing in. They have a Customer Experience team in Dundee (Meadowside) and have advertised Customer Support Advisor roles.

What subscribers deal with today

  • Four numbers: Courier digital (0800 029 4956), Press and Journal digital (0800 029 4955), home delivery (01382 575427) and the DC Thomson Shop for magazines (0800 904 7260).
  • Different hours per line: news digital Mon–Fri 8–18 and Sat 9–17; magazine shop Mon–Fri 9–17 only.
  • Repeat reasons: Trustpilot rates the Shop 4.8/5, but the complaints cluster on late or missing issues, billing frequency, refunds and early renewal reminders.
  • Self-service is already there: a help centre and Air Business subscription management (renewed on a 3-year contract). The tools exist, but customers aren't steered to them.

The pitch in one line: DC Thomson doesn't need another chatbot or contact-centre platform. It needs one front door that steers each subscriber to the fastest fix, so its team can spend more time on the conversations that keep subscribers.

Where the value comes from

  • Fewer avoidable calls. Missing issues, billing dates and pauses move to self-service, around the clock.
  • Shorter queues for the calls that matter. Cancellation and win-back callers reach an agent faster, which protects the £41 M subscription line.
  • Help outside office hours. Subscribers on evenings and Sundays get an answer instead of a closed line.
  • One set of rules for every title. News, magazines and Beano can share a single console, configured per brand.
  • Visibility. Live data on which topics reach which channel, by title. That gives the transformation programme a measurable KPI.

Proof from the Nordics

S-Bank (3.3 M customers, ~1 M interactions a year) uses reChanneld next to its Aulis chatbot to guide customers to the right channel 24/7. reChanneld clients typically guide 79% of contacts to their preferred channel.

What it doesn't require

No new chatbot, no CRM change and no replacement of Air Business. reChanneld sits on top of the help pages that already exist and is configured by the CX team.

Suggested pilot

Weeks 1–2MapPull 3 months of contact reasons from one title, e.g. The Courier, and agree the routing rules.
Weeks 3–8NudgereChanneld goes live on The Courier's help pages, with A/B tests on the top 5 topics.
Week 9DecideCompare call volume, self-service share and evening/weekend use against the baseline, then roll out to other titles.
