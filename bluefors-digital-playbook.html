Homepage audit

FIRST STAGE · REMOVE THE BIGGEST HEAT LOADS

The homepage already looks like a market leader. The opportunities are in conversion paths, consistency and making the site easier for search engines and AI answer engines to understand. Findings are ranked by expected commercial impact.

Keep and build on

  • Ownable visualsGold-plated cryostat photography and "Cool For Progress" are instantly recognisable.
  • Named proofTestimonials carry real names and institutions: The University of Chicago, Silicon Quantum Computing.
  • Fresh newsThree stories in September alone, plus a "Latest" ticker in the hero.
  • Customer logo wallAalto, AIST, Alice & Bob, D-Wave, Fujitsu, IQM, Jülich and more.
High

Two different headline numbers for installed systems

›

Evidence

Homepage: over 1 500 systems installed and fully-operational. Bluefors' LinkedIn company page: more than 1,800 systems delivered.

Why it matters

This is the company's main proof point. AI answer engines and journalists quote whichever number they find first, so two figures weaken both.

Fix

Agree one figure and one definition (delivered vs operational). Store it in a single place and update the site, LinkedIn, press boilerplate and sales decks together.
High

Only one call to action, and it asks for a sales conversation

›

Evidence

The hero offers only Contact Sales. The newsletter appears only as a footer link. Product cards end in Read more.

Why it matters

A physicist scoping a lab or a QPU team planning a roadmap is months from buying. With no mid-funnel offer, these visitors leave anonymous and never reach Salesforce.

Fix

Add tiered offers: "Talk to an application scientist", gated spec sheets or system sizing guides, webinar registration and a newsletter signup in the page body. Score each one in Salesforce.
High

The H1 never says what Bluefors makes

›

Evidence

H1: Delivering the Quantum Future, Today. The words "dilution refrigerator" and "cryogenic" don't appear above the fold.

Why it matters

Search engines and LLMs weight headings heavily when deciding what an entity is. The category Bluefors leads is missing from its most important heading.

Fix

Keep the brand line and add a category subhead, for example "Dilution refrigerators and cryogenic infrastructure for quantum computing". A/B test the version with the category name.
Medium

Four carousels hide most of the content

›

Evidence

Customer logos (3 slides), products (3 visible), testimonials (2 visible) and news all sit behind arrows.

Why it matters

Few visitors click past the first slide, so most logos and products go unseen. Content loaded inside sliders can also be harder for crawlers to read.

Fix

Replace the product slider with a static category grid that matches the menu: dilution refrigerators, 1K/4K systems, measurement infrastructure, cryocoolers, helium management. Check scroll depth and click data in analytics first.
Medium

Homepage speaks only to quantum computing

›

Evidence

The Applications menu lists Low Temperature Physics, Medical and Life Sciences and Clean Energy. None of them appear on the homepage.

Why it matters

Visitors from adjacent markets can't see themselves on the page. The site also misses a chance to segment visitors by need.

Fix

Add an "I'm working on…" chooser (QPU development, physics research, sensing, life sciences). Each path sets a field in the CRM and starts the matching nurture track.
Medium

Strong expertise, not yet packaged for AI answer engines

›

Evidence

The September research article on wiring density and the customer stories hold real expertise. The homepage has no FAQ or explainer content to support them.

Why it matters

Buyers now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews things like "best dilution refrigerator for superconducting qubits". Those tools cite pages that answer in plain, structured language.

Fix

Build a "Cryogenics explained" hub with short Q&A pages, FAQ and Product schema, specs as HTML rather than PDF, and an llms.txt file. Track AI citations monthly against a fixed set of prompts.
Medium

Event block doesn't ask for a meeting

›

Evidence

"NYC Quantum Summit 2026, October 05 – October 06" is listed with a skyline photo and no registration or booking link.

Why it matters

Pre-booked meetings are where event ROI comes from. Without a booking link, the site can't feed the booth calendar.

Fix

Add "Book a meeting with our team" on every upcoming event card, routed into a Salesforce campaign with a 48-hour follow-up rule.
Low

Vague link text, truncated titles and thin body type

›

Evidence

Repeated Read more links. Story links cut off mid-word: Building the Foundations of the Quant…. Body copy is set in a light grey, thin weight.

Why it matters

Descriptive links help screen readers (WCAG 2.4.4) and give search engines anchor text. Low-contrast body text is hard to read on laptops in bright labs.

Fix

Use descriptive link text ("Read the XLDsl system specs"), let titles wrap to two lines, and check body text against WCAG AA contrast.

Scope: this audit is based on a full-page capture of the public homepage. I'd verify page speed, structured data, analytics tagging and CRM form routing with the team in week one. The figures quoted come from Bluefors' own homepage and LinkedIn page.

Measurement framework

PULSE-TUBE STAGE · STABLE BASELINES BEFORE GOING COLDER

One KPI tree from first visit to pipeline, so every channel report answers the same question: did this move a qualified buyer closer to a conversation with Sales? Targets are set after a four-week baseline, not guessed upfront.

My first 90 days

MIXING CHAMBER · WHERE THE WORK HAPPENS
DAYS 1–30 · LISTEN

Map the ecosystem

  • Meet Sales, Product Marketing and IT, and shadow two customer calls
  • Audit Salesforce: lead sources, campaign structure, data quality
  • Set the KPI baseline and the first monthly report
  • Fix quick wins: the systems number, event booking links, link text
DAYS 31–60 · FIX

Build the conversion layer

  • Launch tiered CTAs and lead scoring with Sales sign-off
  • Event playbook: pre-booking, badge-scan routing, 48-hour follow-up
  • Brief the agency on the homepage test for the H1 and product grid
  • Publish the first five "Cryogenics explained" pages
DAYS 61–90 · SCALE

Prove pipeline impact

  • ABM pilot with Sales on 20 named accounts
  • Nurture tracks by application segment
  • GEO tracker live, with a quarterly AI-visibility report
  • Q1 2027 plan built from Q4 results
