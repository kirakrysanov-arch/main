Sales · venues

Email sequence and call script

Juha's toolkit for reaching hotels and event venues. Instructions are in English; every message is in Finnish (to send) with an English translation below it.

Kameli Audio is the venue's go-to sound technician. Many hotels and venues have good equipment but nobody to run it during an event. At company kick-offs and seminars, when a microphone or laptop sound fails, hotel staff try to fix it while also doing their own jobs.

We offer a sound technician on site for the whole event, using the venue's own equipment (or ours when needed). The venue can sell the technician to its clients as an add-on to the meeting package, with Kameli Audio as the one contact.

Sound only. Kameli Audio does not provide lighting. Never offer lighting in emails or calls; if a venue asks, say we focus on sound.

How to use it

  • Who to contact: the meetings and events sales manager (or the hotel's conference manager). Avoid general inboxes (info@) when a named contact exists in the Excel tracker.
  • When: send emails Tue–Thu, 8–10 am. Call 9–11 am or 1–3 pm.
  • How many: 10–15 new venues a week is enough. Quality over quantity.
  • Format: plain text, no attachments in the first email, only one link.
  • Fill in the [bracketed] parts every time: [Etunimi] = first name, [Paikka] = venue name. Rewrite the first personal sentence for each venue.
  • Use your own experience. Real stories from kick-offs you have worked at (without naming the client) are the strongest argument.
  • Stop the sequence as soon as the venue replies, and continue the conversation normally.
  • Log every touch in Kameli_Audio_Venue_Outreach.xlsx: Outreach status, date, Next step and Follow-up date.
  • Seasons: January–February kick-offs are booked in the autumn; Christmas parties (pikkujoulut) are planned Aug–Oct; seminars run all spring and autumn. Mention the nearest season.

The sequence, day by day

  1. Day 0Follow the venue's LinkedIn company page, send a connection request, send Email 1.
  2. Day 3Call 1, if there is no reply.
  3. Day 7Email 2, as a reply in the same email thread.
  4. Day 10LinkedIn message, if the connection was accepted.
  5. Day 14Email 3 (a useful tip).
  6. Day 17Call 2, if there is no reply.
  7. Day 21Email 4 (polite close). Set the status in Excel and try again in 3–4 months.

LinkedIn

  • Follow the venue's company page and like or comment on its posts now and then, so your name is familiar before you message.
  • Send 5–10 connection requests a day at most; more looks unnatural to LinkedIn.
  • After someone accepts, don't sell straight away: thank them first and show interest in their venue.
  • Post short stories from events you have worked at (what went wrong, how it was fixed) – venue sales staff will see them in their feed.

Call script

Don't read it word for word – use it as a structure with ready phrases. The goal of every call is an agreed next step: a meeting, a site visit or a new time to call.

1. Before the call
  • Open the venue's row in the Excel tracker and look at its website: meeting rooms, event types, whether it mentions AV equipment or a technician.
  • Have your calendar open, so you can agree a meeting straight away.
  • Know the one thing you want to find out: who sells meetings and events, and who runs the sound during them.
2. Switchboard

Ask for the person who handles meetings and events sales.

Hei, Juha Pöysä Kameli Audiosta. Kuka teillä vastaa kokous- ja tapahtumamyynnistä?

EN: Hi, Juha Pöysä from Kameli Audio. Who handles meetings and events sales at your venue?

(Jos kysytään syytä:) Haluaisin esitellä ääniteknikkopalvelua, jota voitte tarjota kokousasiakkaillenne.

EN: (If they ask why:) I'd like to introduce a sound technician service you can offer your meeting clients.

Ask for the person's name and a direct number or email.

3. Opener

Ask permission to talk. If it's a bad time, agree a new time.

Hei [Etunimi], Juha Kameli Audiosta. Lähetin viime viikolla sähköpostia – onko sinulla kaksi minuuttia, vai soitanko paremmalla hetkellä?

EN: Hi [First name], Juha from Kameli Audio. I emailed you last week – do you have two minutes, or shall I call at a better time?

4. Reason for calling

Tell your own story briefly – it is the strongest part of the call.

Olen ääniteknikko ja ollut monissa yritysten kick-offeissa hotelleissa. Laitteet ovat yleensä hyvät, mutta kukaan ei hoida niitä tilaisuuden aikana – ja kun jokin menee pieleen, hotellin henkilökunta yrittää korjata. Haluaisimme olla [Paikan] luottoteknikko: ääniteknikko paikalla koko tilaisuuden ajan, teidän laitteillanne.

EN: I'm a sound technician and I've worked at many company kick-offs in hotels. The equipment is usually good, but nobody runs it during the event – and when something goes wrong, hotel staff try to fix it. We'd like to be [Venue]'s go-to technician: a sound technician on site for the whole event, using your equipment.

5. Questions

Ask, then listen. Write the answers in the Excel notes.

Kuka hoitaa äänen tilaisuuksien aikana nyt?

EN: Who runs the sound during events today?

Onko teillä oma teknikko paikalla, vai hoitaako henkilökunta?

EN: Do you have your own technician on site, or do your staff handle it?

Kuinka usein tekniikka aiheuttaa ongelmia tai asiakaspalautetta?

EN: How often does technology cause problems or client complaints?

Millaisia tilaisuuksia teillä on eniten – kick-offeja, seminaareja, juhlia?

EN: What events do you host most – kick-offs, seminars, parties?

Myyttekö tekniikkaa tai teknikkoa osana kokouspakettia?

EN: Do you sell technology or a technician as part of the meeting package?

Kuka muu osallistuu päätökseen?

EN: Who else is involved in the decision?

6. Tailor the offer to the answers
  • Equipment but no technician (the most common case): a technician on site using their equipment; the venue sells it as an add-on.
  • Staff handle the technology: the technician frees staff for their real work (service, schedule, clients) and the client gets a smooth event.
  • They have their own technician: backup for busy days and parallel events, and extra sound equipment for big events.
  • Little or no sound equipment: we bring the sound equipment and the technician.
  • If they ask about lighting: we focus on sound only.
7. Close – ask for the next step

Sopisiko, että tulen käymään [päivä] ja katsotaan tilat ja laitteet yhdessä?

EN: Could I visit on [day] so we can look at the rooms and equipment together?

(Jos ei:) Saanko lähettää lyhyen esittelyn ja soittaa uudelleen [päivämäärä]?

EN: (If not:) May I send a short introduction and call again on [date]?

8. Voicemail

Hei [Etunimi], Juha Pöysä Kameli Audiosta, numeroni on 040 572 7838. Olen ääniteknikko, ja haluaisin kysyä, voisimmeko olla [Paikan] luottoteknikko kokouksissa ja tilaisuuksissa. Lähetän vielä lyhyen sähköpostin ja soitan uudelleen [päivä]. Kiitos!

EN: Hi [First name], Juha Pöysä from Kameli Audio, my number is 040 572 7838. I'm a sound technician, and I'd like to ask whether we could be [Venue]'s go-to technician for meetings and events. I'll send a short email and call again on [day]. Thanks!

9. After the call
  • Update the Excel tracker: status, next step and follow-up date.
  • Send the recap email the same day (template above).
  • Add the right person's name and role to the LinkedIn columns, and send them a connection request.
